# Introduction to Threats and Vulnerabilities

In cybersecurity, protecting digital assets relies on understanding two fundamental concepts: **Vulnerabilities** and **Threats**. While often mentioned together, they represent distinct aspects of system security.

## 1. Vulnerability (The Internal Flaw)
A vulnerability is a weakness, flaw, or bug within a system, network, software, or organizational process that leaves it exposed to harm. Vulnerabilities exist independently of whether anyone is actively trying to exploit them.

* **Key Characteristic:** Internal and controllable—organizations can patch, fix, or mitigate vulnerabilities.
* **Examples:** Outdated software without security patches, weak or default passwords, unencrypted data transmissions, misconfigured firewalls, or lack of employee security training.

## 2. Threat (The External Danger)
A threat is any potential event, actor, or action that has the capability to exploit a vulnerability and compromise the confidentiality, integrity, or availability of an asset.

* **Key Characteristic:** External and largely uncontrollable—threats exist regardless of whether a system is secure.
* **Examples:** Malware (ransomware, spyware), phishing campaigns, rogue insiders, distributed denial-of-service (DDoS) attacks, or cybercriminal groups.

---

## How They Interact: The Concept of Risk

Security risk occurs at the intersection of a threat and a vulnerability:

$$\text{Threat} + \text{Vulnerability} = \text{Risk}$$

* Without a vulnerability, a threat has no pathway to breach your system.
* Without a threat, a vulnerability remains an unexploited weakness.

### Real-World Analogy
Imagine a house with an unlocked back door:
* **Vulnerability:** The unlocked door (a weakness in defense).
* **Threat:** A burglar walking down the street looking for houses to enter (the potential danger).
* **Risk:** The high probability that the burglar discovers the unlocked door and steals property inside.
